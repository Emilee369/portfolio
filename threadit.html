Context & direction

A digital wardrobe for more confident dressing.

Threadit helps people organize the clothes they own, keep track of pieces they want, and bring that context into everyday styling decisions. The Closet is the foundation: a place to browse a personal wardrobe and return to it when deciding what to wear or buy.

Our research pointed to a deeper challenge than finding clothing. People collected style inspiration but struggled to see how a look could work for them, with their body, identity, and existing wardrobe. We began with an outfit-identification idea for Netflix viewers; surveys and interviews led us toward a broader question about confidence.

Our guiding question

How might we help people turn style inspiration and the clothes they own into outfits they feel confident wearing?

Understanding everyday choices

The survey showed the disconnect between inspiration, purchases, and everyday wear. Our qualitative notes helped unpack it: saving a look did not necessarily lead to wearing it, and liking it on someone else did not make it easy to picture on yourself.

The interviews shifted our focus from finding a product to helping people decide whether a look felt right for them. For my Closet work, I translated that intention into clearer access to owned clothing and a way to consider wanted pieces in the context of an existing wardrobe.

From research to my design decisions

Confidence became
a design criterion.

I used the research to ask what each Closet decision could help someone feel more certain about: finding a piece, recognizing what they actually wear, or deciding whether something new belongs in their wardrobe. That intention guided my mid-fidelity exploration and the later addition of Wishlist beside Wardrobe.

My contribution

Designing the closet experience.

I designed Threadit’s Closet page to give users a clearer starting point for dressing and shopping. My work focused on making owned clothing easy to find, bringing wear history into view, and connecting future purchases to a personal wardrobe.

My mid-fidelity exploration

From browsing
to adding a piece.

I explored how users would browse their wardrobe, open a piece to see its details, and add a new item. These mid-fidelity screens show three distinct parts of my Closet exploration, before the final visual design.

I translated the research into three tasks: scan what I own, understand a piece, and add it to my wardrobe. The grid supports browsing, item details provide context, and the add-item form gives each piece a consistent structure. These screens established the foundation before I refined the final Closet.

Refining the Closet

More context.
More considered choices.

After feedback, I added Wishlist beside Wardrobe. The Closet evolved from a view of owned clothing into a place to compare what someone has with what they are considering. Switching between the tabs lets users ask whether a piece fills a gap, duplicates an existing item, or fits the wardrobe they want to build. This iteration connects our confidence goal to a concrete decision, with more context before buying.

Outcome

Best Overall at Design Interactive.

Our team received the Best Overall award in the Design Interactive Spring 2026 cohort. A central intention of the project was to make the transition from research to design decisions and iterations clear, with confidence guiding the experience. My Closet contribution put that intention into practice through easier wardrobe reference and more considered choices about what to add.

What I would test next.

I would ask participants to plan an outfit and consider a purchase using their own clothing. I would look for whether they can find relevant pieces, recognize gaps or duplicates, and explain why a choice feels right for them. Comparing their confidence before and after each task would help assess whether the design supports our intention.

Threadit remains a prototype. Confidence is our design goal; these research findings and iterations do not yet establish a measured improvement in confidence.
